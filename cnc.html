Save Money

DFM Tips That Cut Your CNC Cost

We run this review free on every file before quoting. These are the changes that drop prices most often:

  • Design around stock sizes — standard plate/bar steps (6, 10, 12, 20, 25, 50 mm in aluminum) avoid paying to machine material away.
  • Relax non-critical tolerances — call out ±0.05 mm only on actual fit diameters; leave the rest at ISO 2768-m default.
  • Open up internal corner radii — radius at least 1/3 to 1/2 of pocket depth so larger, faster tools can run.
  • Avoid deep pockets and thin walls — keep pocket depth under 3–4× tool diameter and walls above ~1.5 mm in aluminum.
  • Reduce setups — keep critical features on one side; every flip in the vise adds cost.

FAQ

CNC Machining FAQ

Can't find your answer? Contact us directly.

What file formats do you accept?
STEP is preferred, but we also accept IGES, STL, and native CAD files (SolidWorks, Fusion 360, Inventor). A 2D drawing with critical tolerances helps but is not required — we'll flag anything ambiguous during DFM review.
Is there a minimum order quantity?
No. CNC prototyping starts at 1 piece — no MOQ. Unit price drops significantly at 5, 25, and 100 pieces as setup cost amortizes; we'll show you the price curve in the quote.
What tolerance can you hold?
Standard tolerance is ISO 2768-mK. We hold ±0.05 mm on critical dimensions at no extra process risk — just mark them on your drawing. Tighter than ±0.02 mm is possible on bores and fits; tell us which features matter and we'll confirm feasibility before quoting.
How long does it take?
Typical lead time is 10–14 days from order to your door, including DHL/FedEx shipping to the US, EU, and Australia. Simple parts can ship faster; hardened-steel or plating-heavy jobs run the full 14 days. Your quote always states the committed date.
Do you provide DFM feedback before I order?
Yes — free, on every file, before you pay anything. We flag cost drivers (deep pockets, tight tolerances on non-critical faces, extra setups) and suggest design changes. See our 7 CNC cost-saving design tips for the full method.
